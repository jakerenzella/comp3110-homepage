# The Accelerator

COMP3110 is run like a startup accelerator rather than a typical lecture course. You may join
a small team, have access to industry mentors, identify and pitch a real problem, and move a real machine learning
system from idea to deployment over a single term. Lectures will provide industry lectures, important AI/ML content, and provide lessons learned that should help you through your project.

The term is organised into five phases: a two-week **Onboarding**, three **Sprints**,
and **Demo Day**.

## Onboarding: Weeks 1-2

The first two weeks are a foundations sprint. You form teams, pitch a problem, and
stand up the tooling you will use all term: version control for data and models,
an project tracker, and a deployment target. By the end of Onboarding every
team has a running (if trivial) end-to-end pipeline and a one-page problem framing
that names the stakeholders and what "good" means for them.

The framing matters more than the model. A project that optimises the wrong metric
beautifully is still a failed project.

## Sprint 1: Weeks 3-5

The goal of Sprint 1 is a model or AI tool that runs end-to-end and produces
a number you can beat later. Teams gain access to mentors, scope
the problem down to something achievable, and set up an evaluation that reflects
the real objective.

## Sprint 2: Weeks 7-9

With a baseline in place, Sprint 2 is about improving the parts that
actually move the metric — usually the data, not the model. Teams add proper
evaluation, start monitoring the system's behaviour, and build a deployment path so
the model can be served rather than run by hand in a notebook.

## Sprint 3: Weeks 9-10

The final sprint focuses on the qualities that separate a demo from a system:
design, a landing page, reliability, scaling, cost, and the human factors around an ML product. Teams should
stress-test their deployment, add necessary safety and compliance guardrails.

## Mentors

Every team has access to bookable mentors who can assist with both technical and non-technical problems.

- **One check-in at the start of each sprint** (three in total), where the team
  presents progress and the mentor pressure-tests the plan.
- **Async access** between check-ins through the course forum for pointed questions.
- Mentors are advisors, not markers — they help you make better decisions, and the
  teaching team does the assessment.

## Demo Day format

The term ends with Demo Day. Each team gives a short, live presentation of a
**deployed** system to an audience of peers, mentors, and industry guests, followed
by questions.
