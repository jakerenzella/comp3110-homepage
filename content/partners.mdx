# Partners

UNSW Sydney's COMP3110 is a high-level, elective course for computer science students, run like an accelerator. Small
teams of UNSW students identify a real problem, pitch it, and move a machine learning
system from idea to deployment over a single term. The term ends with Demo Day, where
every team presents a **deployed** system to a live audience.

## A call for mentors

Mentors help teams make better decisions. You are not on the hook for anyone's grade, and you
will never be asked to review work.

**When:** mentoring sessions run between Week 3 and Week 11 of Term 3 (roughly **late
September to late November 2026**). If you would like to be involved, it helps us most if
you can confirm by the end of Week 1 (**19 September**).

As a mentor:

- **You control your availability.** You get a bookable link that syncs with your own
  calendar, so students can only ever book time you are available.
- **You control the volume.** Set a cap on how many sessions you take, per week or
  across the term. Even one session all term is a genuinely useful contribution.
- **Sessions are 30 minutes.** Teams book them when they are stuck on something
  specific, not on a fixed schedule for the sake of it.
- **No preparation.** Teams send a short agenda ahead of the session: where they are,
  what they are stuck on, what they want from you. You read it in two minutes and turn
  up. If a session gets booked and you need the time back, cancel it.

### What type of help will they need?

- "Is this even the right problem to solve?"
- "Our metric looks great and the system feels useless. Why?"
- "We have three weeks left. What do we cut?"
- "How should we structure our demo presentation?"
- "How does this work in a real company? Who would own it? What would break?"
- "Is this a ten million dollar idea?"

### Who we are looking for

You do not need to be a machine learning specialist. Teams need technical mentors
(engineering, data, infrastructure, research) and just as much non-technical ones:
product, design, commercialisation, ethics, philanthropy, societal impact and policy.

## A call for guest lectures

We run guest lectures through the term alongside the core content. The brief is
simple: tell students something true about the work that they will not find in a
textbook.

- **Format:** a full lecture slot or a shorter segment, whichever suits. Q&A is where
  the value tends to be, so we leave real room for it.
- **Location:** in person at UNSW Kensington is ideal.
- **Preparation:** we are not looking for a polished conference talk. Rough slides and
  real examples beat a rehearsed pitch.
- **Recording:** lectures are normally recorded and shared with enrolled students. If
  you would rather speak freely, we can leave the recording off. Just say so.

This is not a recruiting session, though we are perfectly happy for you to mention that
you are hiring.

## Get in touch

Email **cs3110@unsw.edu.au** or **jake.renzella@unsw.edu.au** with a line or two about what you work on and which
of the two you are interested in. If you are unsure whether you would be useful, say
that, and we will work it out together.
